# voyage — the research method

This is Phase 3 of `plan-trip`, and it is the whole of `research-destination`. It
produces one thing: a **dossier with a bibliography**, finished and written down
before any itinerary exists.

Read `rules.md` first. The source hierarchy in § 5, the hard refusals in § 2 and
the risk watchlist in § 9 govern everything below.

---

## 1. Why the dossier is a separate artefact

A model that researches while it composes does not research. It commits to a shape
— seven days, a museum morning, a harbour dinner — then goes looking for things to
fill the holes. When a search comes back thin the hole gets filled from memory
instead, and memory produces venues that shut in 2019, canyons that were never
there, and cable cars that were never built.

Splitting the work removes the hole. Research answers *what exists and what is
true*; composition answers *what order*. By the time you are choosing an order the
set of places is closed, sourced and written down, so there is nothing to invent
with. The rule that enforces it: **no place may appear in the itinerary that is
not in the dossier.** If composing reveals a gap, come back and research the gap.

---

## 2. Before you search

Four things in front of you. If one is missing, get it before spending a search.

| Input | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Today's date | Deadlines are computed backwards from a date of use; every `checked on` stamp needs it |
| The dates, or the season | Weekday closures, holidays, shutdowns and forecast availability hang off this |
| The party | Two adults and a party of six with a toddler get different lodging, buffers and food |
| What they care about, ranked | The lens. Food-led and landscape-led travellers get different dossiers for one town |

`research-destination` runs with less — often a place and a rough month; § 9 says
what changes.

## 3. The six lanes

| # | Lane | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Transport & logistics | Getting there, moving about, transfer times, booking horizons |
| 2 | Lodging | Neighbourhoods, a short candidate list, what is included, what it really costs |
| 3 | Food | Named places, lead times, price bands, market days, dietary reality |
| 4 | Things to do & timing | What to book when, when to go, what shuts, what rain kills |
| 5 | Weather, clothing & seasonality | Normals, forecast, daylight, kit |
| 6 | Practicalities & risk | Money, connectivity, customs, holidays, health, the advisory link |

### Lane 1 — Transport & logistics

**Establish.** How they get there from their stated origin and what it plausibly
costs. How they move between bases, in real journey times rather than distances.
Airport-to-city transfer named as a service, door to door. Which legs sell out or
price up, and the booking horizon for each. Whether a rail or city pass beats
point-to-point, and whether it must be bought before arrival. If a car is needed,
parking and one-way fees. Where the realistic buffers go.

**Search.** `[origin] to [destination] flights [month]` · `[operator] timetable
[route]` · `[city] airport transfer` · `[country] rail pass 2026` · `[route]
booking opens how far ahead` · `[city] car hire one-way fee`.

**Sources.** Tier 4 — the railway, the airline, the ferry company, direct. Never
an aggregator for a timetable or a horizon; they lag and they misreport release
dates. Tier 7 for a national pass explainer. Flight MCP servers where present.

**Returns.** A leg table — from, to, mode, operator, scheduled duration, realistic
door-to-door with the buffer shown separately, frequency, booking horizon,
computed calendar deadline, source URL. Plus prose on anything awkward: a
connection that only runs on weekdays, a ferry that stops in October, a station
with a twenty-minute walk between platforms.

### Lane 2 — Lodging

**Establish.** Which neighbourhoods suit *this* traveller, with a position stated
on each rather than a neutral survey. Then **cut the candidates to two or three
and say which one you would take** — `rules.md` § 8 asks you to narrow hard and
then commit, not to hand back a long list. What is included: breakfast, lift,
kitchen, laundry, aircon, parking. City or tourist tax, almost always charged on
top and often in cash.
Cancellation terms, and the date the free window closes.

**Search.** `[city] where to stay [traveller type]` · `[neighbourhood] at night` ·
`[hotel] official site` · `[city] tourist tax 2026` · `[city] [month] availability`.

**Sources.** The property's own site for facilities and terms. Tier 7 for the tax,
which the tourism board publishes. Below-the-line sources are fine for
neighbourhood *texture*, labelled as such, and never for a price or a policy.

**Returns.** Two or three neighbourhoods, each with one line on who it suits and
one on the trade-off. Two or three properties as item blocks (§ 6), with a named
recommendation and the reason. State the total for the stay, not the nightly rate,
and state what the tax adds.

### Lane 3 — Food

**Establish.** Named places in bands: one or two needing booking now, several good
mid-range, three or four no-reservation fallbacks near where the traveller will
actually be standing. The lead time and release mechanic for anything hard — many
places open a fixed number of days ahead at a fixed local hour. Price bands per
person. Market days and hours, which are weekday-specific and matter. How the
destination handles the traveller's dietary requirement in practice, sourced
rather than assumed. And the local speciality genuinely worth the detour,
distinguished from the one that is merely famous.

**Search.** `[city] reservations open [days] ahead` · `[restaurant] official site
booking` · `[city] market days hours` · `[city] gluten free / vegan / halal` ·
`[dish] where to eat [city] local`.

**Sources.** The restaurant's own site or booking page for hours, price and
release window. Tier 7 for market calendars. Established food publications below
the line, labelled — legitimate for *opinion*, useless for *hours*.

**Returns.** Item blocks with price band, booking mechanic and computed deadline.
A one-line verdict on the speciality with a position taken. A named
no-reservation fallback for each area the traveller will be in — the itinerary
needs one per day and cannot invent them later.

### Lane 4 — Things to do & timing

**Establish.** Candidate anchors, and for each: whether it needs booking, how far
ahead, and whether it is a lottery or a scramble. Weekly closure day. Seasonal
closure — the pass, the hut, the boat, the restaurant that shuts for winter, the
site closed for excavation. Best time of day and why: light, crowd, tide, heat,
coach-party timing. Weather dependence stated bluntly — does drizzle spoil it or
ruin it. And **three named fallbacks per anchor candidate** — one per failure
mode, because rules.md § 3 demands all three and composition cannot conjure them:

- a **weather alternate** — indoor or sheltered, for when the weather turns;
- a **stamina alternate** — where the party goes to sit down when it runs out of
  road, and what gets cut to get them there;
- a **closed-door alternate** — for the strike, the renovation, the private hire
  or the closure day nobody caught.

Each carries its travel time from where the anchor is, because that is where the
plan will be standing when it fails.

**Search.** `[venue] official tickets` · `[venue] opening hours closed day` ·
`[attraction] sell out how far ahead` · `[region] seasonal closures [month]` ·
`[site] best time of day` · `[city] rainy day`.

**Sources.** Tier 6 — the venue's own ticketing site — for hours, closure days,
dress codes and release windows. Resellers misreport all four as a matter of
routine. Tier 7 for holiday calendars. Below the line for crowd timing and light.

**Returns.** Item blocks with confidence, evidence-of-operation year, price,
booking horizon and computed deadline. A closure table keyed to weekday. All
three fallbacks per anchor as their own item blocks, sourced to the same standard
and labelled by failure mode — a backup you cannot evidence is not a backup.

### Lane 5 — Weather, clothing & seasonality

**Establish.** Climate normals for the actual month: mean high and low,
precipitation days, wind, sea temperature where it matters. **And**, if the dates
fall inside forecast range, the actual forecast — with the date you pulled it and
an explicit note that it will move. Daylight: sunrise and sunset for a mid-trip
date, which in the north and in winter decides the shape of every day. Then what
it all means for kit.

**Search.** `[location] climate normals [month]` · `[met service] forecast
[location]` · `[location] sunrise sunset [date]` · `[activity] what to wear`.

**Sources.** Tier 5 — the national met service by name: Met Office, Météo-France,
met.no, JMA, NOAA. WMO for normals and severe-weather warnings. Not a weather app,
and not a blog's remembered impression of a season.

**Returns.** A normals table. A forecast block, dated and captioned provisional,
or an explicit "outside forecast range — normals only". Daylight figures. A kit
list keyed to the activities lane 4 found, not to the season in general —
waterproof trousers matter for the fjord boat, not for the city.

### Lane 6 — Practicalities & risk

**Establish.** Cash or card, and where the exceptions bite: the market stall, the
mountain hut, the taxi. Connectivity — eSIM availability, coverage gaps, whether
public wifi is usable. Plug type and voltage. Tipping norms, stated as norms and
not rules. Transport etiquette, and the ticket-validation trap that fines
tourists. Named scam patterns and the specific squares and stations where they
run. Dress codes at religious sites, and where entry is actually refused without
them. **Public holidays falling inside the dates**, and what shuts. Health: tap
water, pharmacies, altitude, water safety, wildlife. Emergency numbers. And the
official government advisory, linked.

**Search.** `[country] cash or card 2026` · `[country] public holidays [year]` ·
`[city] common tourist scams` · `[site] dress code` · `[country] emergency number`
· `[country] travel advice` on the traveller's own government site.

**Sources.** Tier 1 for the advisory, Tier 2 for health, Tier 7 for the holiday
calendar, Tier 4 for validation rules. Below the line for scam texture, labelled —
neither the police nor the tourism board publishes it, so recent forum reports are
the honest source and should be treated as dated opinion.

**Returns.** A short, scannable practicalities block. A holiday table with dates
and what closes. A named risk list. The advisory URL, and nothing else on entry —
see § 10.

## 4. Dispatching the lanes

**Parallel, where the environment supports subagents.** One per lane. Give each
the confirmed brief, today's date, the relevant part of rules.md and the item
format from § 6. Tell each to return sourced findings rather than prose essays,
and not to make recommendations outside its lane.

**Sequential, where it does not.** Six lanes one after another in a single context
is the point at which quality collapses in the middle. Do not attempt all six. Run
four, in this priority:

1. **Things to do & timing** — the anchors, the closures, and the deadlines that
   expire. Everything else arranges itself around what this lane finds.
2. **Transport & logistics** — the other expiring deadlines, and the mistakes
   that cost most to unwind.
3. **Weather, clothing & seasonality** — it decides what the other lanes'
   findings are worth: daylight, the season the boat runs, the day the rain
   kills. For `research-destination` it is usually the question being asked.
4. **Practicalities & risk** — safety, public holidays, emergency numbers and
   the official advisory link, which rules.md § 2 requires be present.

**Food and lodging drop first**, because both are the most recoverable close to
the trip: dinner can be found on the day, and a bed can be booked from a phone in
an evening. **Weather is never the first lane dropped.** The old instinct — you
can buy a coat — is wrong the moment the trip is outdoors, at altitude, on water
or at latitude in winter, and it is wrong for any trip whose days are shaped by
daylight or a seasonal closure.

Two exceptions, both narrow. If food is the traveller's stated lens, run food as
a deliberately narrow fifth pass: the one or two places that need booking now,
and a no-reservation fallback for each area. If the traveller still has to choose
where to sleep, run lodging too, narrowed to a single neighbourhood and a single
property rather than the two-or-three shortlist `rules.md` § 8 normally wants.

Whatever you drop, **say so at the top of the dossier and again in the delivered
plan**: which lanes ran, which did not, and what the traveller should therefore
check themselves. A thinner document delivered silently is the worse failure.

## 5. Search discipline

**Four to eight searches per lane. Ten is the hard ceiling.** Below four you are
guessing. Nine and ten are allowed only when something genuinely contested sits in
the lane and the extra searches are spent resolving it. Past ten you are
re-reading the same content farm, so stop: an unresolved disagreement belongs in
the conflicts section, not in an eleventh search.

**Stop on saturation.** Two consecutive searches returning nothing new means the
lane is done. Write it up.

**Do not lean on one domain.** If the same site supports more than about three
claims in a lane, you have researched one page rather than a subject. The failure
this prevents: an aggregator restates a venue's hours wrongly, you cite it three
times, and the day is built on one stale copy.

**Search for the negative.** `[venue] closed` and `[venue] permanently closed`
cost one search each and catch the most damaging class of error. Run them for
every anchor candidate.

**Existence and currency are two searches.** An item needs a current-year signal
— a dated menu, a this-season timetable, an events page for the right year —
before it can be `HIGH`.

## 6. Dossier format

Its own document, before any itinerary.

```markdown
# Dossier — [destination] · [dates or season]
Researched [YYYY-MM-DD] · Mode: [6 lanes parallel | 4 lanes sequential — dropped X, Y]
Brief: [one line — who, how long, what they are chasing]

## Headlines
Five things that change the shape of the trip: deadlines already tight, closures
inside the dates, anything meaning the brief needs revisiting.

## 1. Transport & logistics   [leg table] [awkward-connection notes]
## 2. Lodging                 [neighbourhoods positioned] [2–3 item blocks] [the one I'd take]
## 3. Food                    [item blocks by band] [market days] [diet] [speciality verdict]
## 4. Things to do & timing   [anchor candidates + 3 fallbacks each] [weekday closure table]
## 5. Weather & seasonality   [normals] [forecast, dated, or "outside range"] [daylight] [kit]
## 6. Practicalities & risk   [money, comms, plugs, tipping, scams, dress] [holidays] [health]
                              [emergency numbers] [advisory link]
## Conflicts      [§ 7]
## Gaps           What could not be established, and where to look
## Bibliography   [§ 8]
```

Every named place is an item block carrying the Tier 1 fields from `ledger.md`:

```
**[Name]** · [type] · [one-line character]
[Street address, local script where the taxi driver needs it]
[primary source URL] · [tier marker]
Price: [range] [currency] pp (seen YYYY-MM-DD)
Booking: [horizon and mechanic] → deadline [calendar date]
Evidence of operation: [year, and what evidenced it]
Hours: confirm before you go — [official link]
Confidence: HIGH | MEDIUM | LOW
Earns its slot because: [one line, in the traveller's terms]
```

`LOW` items are dropped here, not carried forward with a hedge. Drop it and find
something you can stand behind.

## 7. Conflicts

Two lanes will disagree. Two sources inside one lane will disagree. The ferry
operator's timetable and the tourism board's summary of it will not match, and one
of them is six months old.

**Record both. Name which you followed and why. Never silently pick.**

```
### Conflict — Bergen–Flåm ferry, last autumn sailing
- [T4] Operator timetable: daily to 30 September
- [T7] Tourism board: "runs through October"
Followed the operator — tier 4 beats tier 7, and the timetable is dated this
season. Flagged as confirm-before-booking.
```

Where tier cannot resolve it, say so and take the conservative reading: the
earlier closure, the shorter window, the higher price. Then flag it for the
traveller to confirm. An unresolved conflict is a finding; concealing it is the
failure.

## 8. Bibliography

Every claim that can expire is traceable to a line here — prices, hours,
timetables, holiday closures, booking horizons, forecasts. One entry per source,
tier-marked against rules.md § 5.

```
[T4] Vy — Bergen Line timetable
     https://www.vy.no/...
     Supports: Oslo S 08:25 → Bergen 14:59 daily; seat reservation compulsory
     Checked: 2026-08-01

[B]  Below-the-line food blog
     https://...
     Supports: opinion only — that the fish soup at [place] is the one worth having
     Checked: 2026-08-01
```

Count the entries and report the number. Then count the expiring claims with no
entry behind them and **report that number too**. A bibliography covering nine
claims out of forty is a document pretending to be sourced.

**Never write a URL you have not seen in a search result.** A plausible dead link
is worse than no link, because it stops the traveller looking.

---

## 9. When there are no dates

`research-destination` usually runs on a place and a rough month. Three changes:

- Lane 5 gives normals only, and says so — no forecast, and the month's daylight
  range rather than a figure for a specific day.
- Lane 4 gives weekly closure days and seasonal windows, but no weekday check and
  no computed deadlines. Give the horizon in days; the arithmetic waits for dates.
- Lane 6 gives the month's holiday calendar rather than the dates'.

Everything else runs unchanged, including the item standard and the bibliography.

---

## 10. What research must not do

**No itinerary shaping.** Do not order the days, group places into mornings, or
write "day one". The moment a lane starts sketching a day it starts selecting
evidence to fit it.

**No date assignment**, not even provisionally. Composition assigns dates, after
the pacing law has been applied to the whole set.

**No entry facts.** Visas, ESTA, ETIAS, eTA, K-ETA, passport validity, vaccination
mandates, customs allowances, pet imports. Do not state the requirement, not even
when a search result states it plainly and you are certain. Name the official
source, link it, tell the traveller to check it. Hard refusal, rules.md § 2.

**No filling a gap you could not source.** If lane 4 returns two anchor candidates
for a four-day trip, the finding is "two anchors, here is the gap" — not three,
one of which you remembered.

---

## 11. Failure modes to watch for in yourself

Stop and correct if you catch yourself about to:

- Write a venue name you have not searched for in this session
- Cite an aggregator for an opening time, a timetable or a booking horizon
- Smooth over a disagreement between two sources instead of recording it
- Return prose that reads well and contains no addresses, prices or URLs
- Return a long list of candidates when `rules.md` § 8 wants it cut to two or
  three with one of them backed
- Run six lanes sequentially and not mention that they went shallow
- Let a `LOW` item through with a caveat attached
- Sketch a day
